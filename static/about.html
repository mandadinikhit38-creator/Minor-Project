THE BASICS

What is phishing?

Phishing is a form of social engineering where an attacker pretends to be a trusted person or organization to trick someone into revealing information, sending money, or opening a harmful link or file. Messages often create urgency, ask for secrecy, or direct you to a look-alike website.

PhishLens is an informational URL-checking tool. It combines address and domain signals with available DNS, certificate, page, and threat-feed information to help you assess suspicious links before visiting them. Results are estimates: websites can change, data sources may be unavailable, and no automated scan can guarantee that a site is safe.

COMMON TACTICS

Types of phishing.

Know the tactics. Pause before you respond.

Email phishing

Bulk emails imitate familiar companies or services and ask you to click a link, open an attachment, or sign in.

Spear phishing

A targeted message uses details about a specific person or team to seem personal and trustworthy.

Whaling

A targeted scam aimed at executives or others with authority to approve payments or share sensitive data.

Smishing and vishing

Smishing uses SMS or messaging apps; vishing uses phone or voice messages to pressure you into acting.

Clone phishing

A real message is copied, but its attachment or link is replaced with a malicious version.

QR phishing (quishing)

A QR code hides a link that may lead to a fake sign-in page or an unsafe download.

Pharming and look-alike domains

Traffic is redirected or a deceptive address is used to make a fake website appear legitimate.

Protect yourself: check the full sender and domain, avoid unexpected attachments, don’t share passwords or one-time codes, and verify urgent payment requests through a separate trusted channel. When in doubt, use the organization’s official app or website.

A safe scan result does not guarantee that a website is harmless. Avoid entering sensitive information on sites you do not trust.
